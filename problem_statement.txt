Financial institutions incur substantial losses due to loan defaults. Traditional classification models optimize metrics such as accuracy or ROC-AUC, which do not directly reflect economic impact.

In credit risk decision-making:

Approving a borrower who later defaults generates a loss proportional to Loss Given Default (LGD).

Rejecting a creditworthy borrower results in forgone interest income.

This project reframes credit risk modeling as an economic decision optimization problem.

Business Objective

Develop a binary classification system that:

Estimates probability of default (PD).

Converts PD into approve/reject decisions via thresholding.

Optimizes the threshold to maximize expected portfolio profit.

Evaluates policy robustness under stressed economic conditions.

Economic Framework

Let:

π = profit per performing loan

L = loss given default

TN(t) = performing loans approved

FN(t) = defaulted loans approved

Total profit under threshold t:

Profit(t) = TN(t) × π − FN(t) × L

The objective is:

t* = argmaxₜ Profit(t)

Stress Testing

To simulate recessionary conditions, LGD is increased and the optimal threshold is re-derived, allowing the approval policy to adapt to changing economic risk.
